# Handgroove

Music you play with your hands. Five sounds float on the screen like lights; where you place them is how they play.
Everything is generated live in your browser, and the camera image never leaves your computer.

**Play it:** https://flatfrank.github.io/handgroove/ — open it in Chrome on a laptop and allow the camera.
The music is already running: click anywhere or press any key once to hear it (browsers require that first click).
No camera? Just drag the lights with the mouse. Press **0** at any time for help.

## Your hands

| Do this | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Left hand: make a shape** | Chooses a sound. Fist = **Kick** · 1 finger = **Ticks** · 2 = **Sub** · 3 = **Pad** · 4 = **Bells** |
| **Right hand: close your fist** | Grabs the chosen sound. It follows your hand. |
| **Open your right hand** | Drops it there. It keeps playing that way. New sounds fade in over a few seconds. |
| **Drop it past the edge** | Fades it out. Grab it back in and it fades back in. |
| **Open hands** | Do nothing, so you can rest. |

While you hold a sound, you can also **push your hand toward the camera or pull it back**, and **twist your wrist** like a knob.

## The five panels

Press **1** to **5** (or click the tabs at the top right). Every sound has a place on panels 1–3 and 5.

| | Panel | Left → right | Bottom → top | Push / pull | Twist |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Sound** | sparse → dense | dark → bright | near and dry → far and washed out | instrument |
| **2** | **Texture** | smooth → granular | clean → dusty | less → more texture | — |
| **3** | **Style** | loose → locked | organic → industrial | louder → quieter | — |
| **4** | **Harmony** | (free) | dark mood → bright mood | one chord → many chords | key |
| **5** | **Rhythm** | on the beat → off the beat | even → odd groups | frozen → evolving | interlock |

- **Sound**: twist a held light like a rotary switch to change its instrument. Kick: kick, tom, conga · Ticks: ticks, shaker, clap · Sub: sub, reese, 808 · Pad: pad, organ, strings · Bells: bells, marimba, pluck.
  The new instrument comes in on the next beat, and everything else you set for that light stays.
- **Style**: organic is wood, skin and breath; industrial is metal, machines and grit. The far top-right corner
  (most locked and most industrial) is the **acid zone**: resonance and sliding basslines grow as you push into it.
  Loose patterns change every bar; locked patterns repeat.
- **Harmony** has a single light, the key. Grab it with your right fist alone. Changes land on the next bar.
  Moving it sideways does nothing, so you can't change the tempo by accident.
- **Rhythm**: the sounds start on the left edge with free patterns. Bring one into the field to give it a rhythm of its own.
  Left = on the beat, right = off the beat (syncopated). Bottom = even groups (4+4+4+4), top = odd ones (3+3+2, 5+3…).
  The ring of 16 dots around each sound is its bar; the lit dot is the step playing now. How many notes it plays still
  comes from the Sound panel. Pull back while holding and the pattern keeps evolving; push in and it freezes.
  Twist for **interlock**: the parts start listening to each other, so the bass avoids the kick, the ticks fill the gaps
  and the bells answer. There are no genres here: they appear from the mix. Drop a sound past the edge for its free pattern again.
- On the Texture, Style, Harmony and Rhythm panels, dropping a light past the edge resets it.

## Mouse and keyboard

- **Drag** a light to move it.
- **Scroll** on a light: the instrument on the Sound panel · **Shift + scroll**: the push/pull control of the current panel.
- **Tempo**: **↑ / ↓** arrow keys, 1 BPM per press; **Shift + ↑ / ↓** for 10 BPM (60–180). It eases to the new speed.
- On the Harmony panel: scroll changes the key, Shift + scroll the number of chords.
- On the Rhythm panel: scroll changes the interlock, Shift + scroll the mutation.
- **1–5** panels · **0** help · **[ ]** overall filter · **H** if your hands seem swapped.

## Tips

- Sit about an arm's length from the camera, with your hands well lit and in view.
- Start with the pad and sub already playing, then bring in the kick (fist) and ticks (1 finger).
- Nothing changes at once: every sound glides to where you put it over a few seconds, on every panel. Rhythms move a note
  or two per bar, the tempo eases to its new speed, and key changes land on the next bar.
- Moves are smoothed and land in time, so slow, deliberate gestures sound best.
